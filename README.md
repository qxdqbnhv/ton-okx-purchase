### 4. Decide between a market order and a limit order

A market order is designed to execute immediately at the best available prices in the order book. It is convenient, but the final average price can differ from the figure visible when the order is placed. Several order-book levels may be used to complete the trade.

A limit order lets you specify the maximum price you are willing to pay. It may remain open until the market reaches that price. A limit order is not automatically a maker order: if it matches existing liquidity immediately, it can still be charged at the taker rate.

For a first small purchase, a market order is easier to understand. For a larger order, a limit order can give you more control, but it does not guarantee execution.

## Common mistakes when buying TON

### Mistaking the token ticker

TON is the ticker commonly used for Toncoin, but crypto platforms can have similarly named assets or unrelated tickers. Confirm the asset name and network before buying or withdrawing.

The finance ticker symbol used by one market-data provider may not correspond to Toncoin, so rely on the asset page and trading pair inside OKX rather than typing a ticker into a third-party app and assuming the result is correct.

### Assuming the displayed price is guaranteed

Crypto prices can move between quote, order submission, and execution. A market order can fill at several prices, particularly when the market is volatile or liquidity is limited.

### Treating a limit order as guaranteed

A limit order controls price, not execution. If the market never reaches your limit, the order can remain open.

### Ignoring the spread on Buy/Sell

A route with no separately displayed commission is not necessarily free. OKX says the Buy/Sell and Convert price includes the cost in the quoted price and spread.

### Sending TON over the wrong network

This is a transfer mistake, not a trading mistake, but it is more expensive to fix. Confirm network compatibility before withdrawing.

### Using leverage when the goal is simply to buy TON

Spot buying and leveraged derivatives are different activities. If your goal is to own TON, a spot purchase is easier to understand and avoids liquidation mechanics. Futures and perpetual products may involve funding fees and liquidation risk. OKX distinguishes these costs from normal spot trading fees.
